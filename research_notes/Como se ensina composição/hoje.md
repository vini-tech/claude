# How composition and stylistic writing are taught today (c.1990–2026): schools, exams, pedagogy research and exercise formats

Note on method: 19 tool calls (searches and fetches). Many official pages (Oxford handbook, CNSMDP curriculum, HMTM PDF) could not be read in full: PDFs came back compressed or results were indirect. Claims are cited to what was actually retrieved. Items known from background knowledge but NOT verified in this session are listed under Gaps or marked "(unverified)" in Inferences.

## 1. Stylistic composition / pastiche exams (UK, Germany/Austria, France, Italy)

### Takeaway
Across Europe the core graded exercises are much the same: Bach-style four-part chorale on a given melody, unfigured or figured bass realization (4 parts), two-part invention / 18th-c. two-part counterpoint, 16th-c. cantus-firmus counterpoint, and a dance (minuet). Higher levels add fugue, string quartet continuation and orchestration. Most of it is assessed through timed or take-away "complete the given fragment" papers and portfolios. The UK has moved from timed papers toward portfolios and coursework. Italy is still dominated by the "basso dato" (given unfigured bass, 4 voices, with modulations).

### Cited Findings
**UK: Cambridge**
- Part IA "Tonal Skills 1" is compulsory. It covers "tonal" music from Barbara Strozzi to Brahms, plus jazz and film music, and teaches keyboard harmony, aural work and "writing music in a range of historical styles". Part IB "Applied Tonal Skills" (stylistic composition, fugue, orchestration, film score) ends in a coursework portfolio. Part II offers Advanced Tonal Skills, Fugue, Advanced (keyboard) Skills and a Composition Portfolio as options — [Cambridge Music Course](https://www.mus.cam.ac.uk/cambridge-music-course)
- 1999 Part IA keyboard tests: reading a string-quartet score, transposing a chorale, harmonising a given melody in a pre-1828 style, realising a figured bass, and reading four-part 16th-c. counterpoint in four clefs — [Cambridge Reporter 1998-99](https://www.admin.cam.ac.uk/reporter/1998-99/weekly/5783/21.html)
- 2008–09 Part IA Paper 2 ("Harmony and counterpoint II"): a string-quartet question in which candidates continue a given opening for up to 40 bars. The paper's working time was cut from 48 to 24 hours, i.e. a take-away paper — [Cambridge Reporter 2008-09](https://rso.admin.cam.ac.uk/reporter/2008-09/weekly/6128/20.html)
- The 2018–19 statutes list Part IA papers "Introduction to music analysis" (Paper 5), "Tonal skills 1" (Paper 6) and "Tonal skills 2" — [Statutes and Ordinances 2018](https://www.admin.cam.ac.uk/univ/so/2018/chapter04-section29.html)

**UK: Oxford**
- The Prelims (first year) handbook for 2017–18 lists "Techniques of Composition" as a compulsory paper, assessed as a 7-day take-away paper and paired with a Keyboard Skills short test — [St Edmund Hall Prelims Handbook 2018](https://www.some.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Prelims-Handbook-2018-web.pdf)
- Anecdotal (forum, applicant level): for the harmonisation submission, most people harmonise the lower three parts of a Bach chorale under a given soprano — [The Student Room](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showpost.php?p=99828646)
- Related UK university practice: Manchester's first-year course starts with Palestrina-style two-part writing as context for Bach, and also covers chorale harmonisation in the style of J. S. Bach — [Manchester MUSC10112](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/study/undergraduate/courses/2025/02397/musb-music/course-details/MUSC10112)
- A study of "techniques of composition" in the British and Irish university curriculum exists (British Journal of Music Education); only the abstract page was found — [BJME article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-music-education/article/abs/techniques-of-composition-in-the-british-and-irish-university-curriculum/725DB83556C4961B957D9DDE00DA58B3)

**UK: A-level (Pearson Edexcel 9MU0) and ABRSM**
- Edexcel A-level Component 2: the second composition is a "Brief assessing technique" worth 20 marks. The four options are **Bach chorale, Two-part counterpoint, Arrangement, Remix**. Students have "up to six hours under controlled conditions" (the guidance says 4–6 hours). Briefs are released on 1 April — [Pearson Briefs guidance](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Music/2016/teaching-and-learning-materials/a-level-music-Briefs-assessing-technique-guidance.pdf); [2018 brief](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A-Level/Music/2016/Exam-materials/58367_GCE_A_Music_9MU0_02_Composition_Brief_Jun_2018.pdf)
- Wording of the chorale brief: "add alto, tenor and bass parts to harmonise the following TWO chorales in the style of J. S. Bach…", at a fixed tempo of 60 crotchets/min. The required duration changes each year (2019: chorale 2'10"; two-part counterpoint 2'05"). The combined minimum is 6 minutes — [2019 brief](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A-Level/Music/2016/Exam-materials/59280-gce-a-music-9mu0-02-composition-brief-jun-2019-new.pdf)
- ABRSM theory: Grades 1–5 were modernised. Multiple-choice items were added and rhythm-writing, melody-writing, word-setting and Grade 5 SATB short/open-score questions were removed. The exams are now online, multiple-choice and can be taken at any time. Grades 6–8 are unchanged and paper-based. About 50% of the paper is writing music. Q1–2 test harmony: key and modulations, cadences, progressions and correct interpretation of figured bass, which is ABRSM-specific. Source is a tutor site (secondary) — [MyMusicTheory ABRSM](https://mymusictheory.com/exam-boards/abrsm/)
- One teacher reports that after Grade 5, candidates now arrive at Grade 6 with no composition skills and little exposure to harmony, SATB or figured bass (opinion) — [MyMusicTheory](https://mymusictheory.com/exam-boards/abrsm/)

**Germany / Austria: Tonsatz**
- In German Hochschule Tonsatz teaching, style copies (Stilkopien) and analyses of Bach chorales are among the most widespread exercises (book review) — [GMTH proceedings](https://storage.gmth.de/proceedings/articles/108/pdf/gmth-proceedings-artikel-108.pdf)
- Mozarteum (MOZ, Salzburg) BA Musiktheorie entrance sample: figured bass (Generalbass), a chorale in Bach style, a two-part invention *or* a cantus-firmus setting in Renaissance style, and the analysis of a piano piece — [MOZ Prüfungsbeispiel](https://www.moz.ac.at/Downloads-Studierende/Pr%C3%BCfungsbeispiele/musiktheorie_pruefungsbeispiel_ba.pdf)
- HMTM entrance exam (Stilübung, as summarised by search; the PDF could not be read directly): candidates choose 2 of at least 5 epoch-specific tasks, such as 16th-c. counterpoint, invention, fughetta, chorale setting and 18th-c. minuet, and must show they can imitate a specific style — [HMTM Musiktheorie-Gehörbildung](https://hmtm.de/wp-content/uploads/Studium/Eignungspruefung/Musiktheorie-Gehoerbildung_AB.pdf)
- Peter-Cornelius-Konservatorium Mainz entrance: two written papers of 60 minutes each. They cover scales, chords, intervals, simple cadences, a four-part homophonic setting of a given melody, harmonic analysis and realisation of a simple figured bass. Parallel 5ths/8ves are forbidden — [PCK Mainz](https://pck-mainz.de/media/documents/studiengang/anforderungen-aufnahmeprüfung-tonsatz-gehörbildung.pdf)
- Church music (HKM Tübingen): organ chorale preludes from the Orgelbüchlein, hymnbook chorale settings (Choralbuchsätze) and chorale improvisation — [HKM Tübingen](https://www.kirchenmusikhochschule.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/HKM_Tuebingen_Aufnahmepruefungsbedingungen_BAx3aMA-Kirchenmusik.pdf)
- Hanns Eisler Berlin publishes a model Tonsatz entrance exam with solutions; it starts from clefs, intervals and keys — [HfM Berlin Muster-Klausur](https://www.hfm-berlin.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Studium/Bewerbung/Zugangspruefung_Muster-Klausur_Tonsatz.pdf); [Lösungen](https://www.hfm-berlin.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Studium/Bewerbung/Zugangspruefung_Muster-Klausur_Tonsatz_Lo__sungen.pdf)

**France: écriture at the CNSMD**
- Historically, the "classes d'écriture" grouped harmony, counterpoint and fugue, and future composers passed through them before entering the composition class (search summary of a regional conservatoire's rules) — [Règlement des études écriture (B. Rattini)](https://www.brunorattini.com/gallery/R%C3%A8glement%20des%20%C3%A9tudes%20%C3%A9criture.pdf)

The diploma is named "Diplôme de Formation Supérieure d'écriture" (possibly an older title) — [Superprof profile](https://www.superprof.fr/professeur-conservatoire-diplome-cnsmd-paris-master-ecriture-prix-enseigne-ecriture-composition-harmonie.html)
- Regional conservatoires (e.g. Saint-Maur) run écriture cycles that prepare for the CNSMD Paris/Lyon entrance competitions. The PDF could not be parsed — [Saint-Maur Écriture](https://www.saint-maur.com/fileadmin/user_upload/2025/Ecriture.pdf)
- HEM Geneva also has a "pratique d'écriture" discipline inside its composition department — [HEM Genève](https://hesge.ch/hem/disciplines/composition/pratique-ecriture)

**Italy: armonia e contrappunto**
- Foggia Triennio "Teoria dell'armonia e analisi" 2022/23. Year 1: a 4-part bass in close position (12 bars in 4/4 or 20–22 bars in 2/4 or 2/2) with modulations to closely related keys, 2 hours, plus an oral. Year 2: a bass with near modulations, suspensions (ritardi), sequences (progressioni), one modulation to a distant key, and analysis of a figured chorale by J. S. Bach — [Conservatorio Foggia](https://www.conservatoriofoggia.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/TEORIA-DELLARMONIA-E-ANALISI-TRIENNIO-2022.23.pdf)
- Verona: the Triennio admission minimum is a 4-part harmonisation of a given bass, without fioriture, with near modulations, in 3 hours. The propedeutic course follows "basso continuo practice and chorale style" — [Conservatorio Verona](https://www.conservatorioverona.it/_upload/_allegato/1581253391.39_283.TeoriaAnalisi-propedeutico.pdf)
- Santa Cecilia (Rome) admission: a 4-part bass with near modulations and melodic ornamental notes, plus analysis of a short piano piece — [Santa Cecilia programmi](https://conservatoriosantacecilia.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/COTP_01-PROGRAMMI-di-studio-Tri_BIennio.pdf)
- Campobasso: written papers without piano. One is a 4-voice realisation of an *unfigured* bass in open spacing *or* a chorale harmonisation respecting Bachian counterpoint; the other is an analytical questionnaire — [Consmi](https://www.consmi.it/getFile.php?id=569)
- Ravenna: 4 hours, unfigured given bass on a double staff with modulations, plus harmonic and non-chord-tone analysis of a Bach chorale — [Verdi Ravenna](https://www.verdiravenna.it/wp-content/uploads/Armonia_programma_propedeutico-1.pdf)
- Perugia: the propedeutic final exam (also the Triennio admission) is an oral analysis of a Bach chorale — [Conservatorio Perugia](https://www.conservatorioperugia.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Programma-Corso-Propedeutico-di-Armonia.pdf)
- Bologna University "Elements of Harmony and Counterpoint": a 2-hour written test writing about 8 bars of florid (5th-species-like) counterpoint above a given part that carries harmonic figures. The oral covers harmony, syntax and form in Bach organ chorales — [UniBo 78048](https://www.unibo.it/en/study/course-units-transferable-skills-moocs/course-unit-catalogue/course-unit/2026/469009)
- Malta matriculation: complete a two-part passage in 18th-c. style and a two-part imitative passage, with one part given in each — [MATSEC syllabus](https://secure.um.edu.mt/media/um/docs/directorates/matsec/syllabi/2015/am/Syllabus20-20AM202420Music202015.pdf)
- Textbook for the chorale-to-invention sequence: Malcolm Boyd, *Bach: Chorale Harmonization and Instrumental Counterpoint*. It moves through graded chorale exercises (cadences, unessential notes, consecutive 5ths/8ves, textures, modal chorales) and then to instrumental counterpoint modelled on the Inventions — [Kahn & Averill](https://www.kahnandaverill.co.uk/product/bach-chorale-harmonization-instrumental-counterpoint/)

### Inferences
- The common European sequence is: (1) given-bass / given-melody 4-part harmony → (2) Bach chorale style → (3) figured bass → (4) 2-part invention / 18th-c. counterpoint and 16th-c. cantus-firmus counterpoint → (5) fugue (exposition, then whole) → (6) quartet continuation / Lied / orchestration in portfolio. The app already covers pieces of steps 1, 3 and the 16th-c. strand, so the obvious gaps are full SATB chorale on a given soprano, unfigured bass, invention, fugue exposition and quartet/minuet continuation.
- The dominant exam *format* is "fragment given → complete it in style", for example the soprano of a chorale, a bass, the opening of an invention, or the first bars of a quartet. This format can be automated: given parts are fixed and the student fills slots, so rule-checking can be applied per voice pair.
- Italy's "basso dato senza numeri" with set lengths (12 bars of 4/4 and so on) and requirements (near modulation + one distant modulation + suspensions + sequences) is a ready-made spec for a graded exercise generator.
- (unverified) The CNSMDP écriture tradition is known for highly stylised "chant donné / basse donnée" harmony in 19th-c. and later idioms (Fauré, Ravel and Debussy styles) and for the "fugue d'école" in 4 voices written in a timed loge exam. Confirm this on cnsmdp.fr before citing.

### Gaps
- No current Oxford past papers or syllabus content (Bach chorale, invention, Palestrina motet, Lieder pastiche) were retrieved. The Oxford/Cambridge pastiche styles named in the brief remain unconfirmed by primary sources in this session.
- No official CNSMDP curriculum page was retrieved. Cycle lengths, the exam format (loge hours, chant donné, fugue d'école) and current prize structure are unverified.
- HMTM and Saint-Maur PDFs could not be decoded (compressed binary).
- AQA/Eduqas A-level and Trinity theory specs were not checked.

## 2. US sequences, textbooks and "model composition" assignments

### Takeaway
No primary syllabi for Juilliard, Curtis, Eastman or Yale were found. What surfaced instead is the textbook landscape (Aldwell & Schachter's Schenker-influenced voice-leading approach; Laitz's integrated composition, analysis, aural and performance approach) and well-documented "style/model composition" assignments: a minuet from Riepel and Mozart's K.1–7 (Eckert), galant composition via Koch and schemata as the organising principle of a 2-year core (Birson), and MIT's minuet-and-trio for string quartet plus a Romantic Lied.

### Cited Findings
- Aldwell & Schachter's *Harmony and Voice Leading* pushed undergraduate theory texts toward Schenkerian ideas. It stresses linear as much as harmonic aspects and introduces large-scale linear and harmonic progressions early — [JMTP review](https://journals.ou.edu/jmtp/article/download/689/1530/1520); [Goodreads listing](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/3061334)
- Laitz's *The Complete Musician* integrates composition, analysis, aural recognition and performance. Reviewers single out this integration as its best feature — [MUSE review](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/550462)
- Stefan Eckert (MTO 11.2, 2005) runs a minuet workshop for second-semester theory students, built on Riepel's *Anfangsgründe* and Mozart's K.1–7 (transposed to C):
  1. Analyse the minuets with a comparison sheet.
  2. Work from reduced 16-bar (8+8) versions, with handouts on "Minuet Extensions", "Cadential Patterns" and "Continuation Patterns (Monte, Fonte, Ponte)".
  3. Diagnose the errors in Riepel's student ("Discantista") minuet.
  4. Do worksheets 1–6: complete the cadences; complete the Fonte and the tonic cadence of part 2; write a melody above a given bass; fill in the opening, modulation to V, Fonte and cadence preparation; complete a minuet from given bars 1–4; write a whole minuet from instructions.
  5. Final project: an expanded minuet (more than 16 bars) composed and performed as a class.

  The constraints follow Riepel: even bar counts, parts of no more than 8 bars, clear 2+2/4-bar groupings, quarter-note motion with dotted notes saved for cadences, part 2 reusing part-1 material, contrast between bars 4 and 5, and 4×4-bar sections (I opening → V-directed → continuation pattern → tonic cadence) with 2-bar cadences (pre-dominant + cadential 6/4, then the goal chord) — [Eckert, MTO 11.2](https://www.mtosmt.org/classic/mto.05.11.2/mto.05.11.2.eckert.html)
- Adem Merter Birson (JMTP) argues that galant-style composition using Koch's *Versuch* and voice-leading schemata can organise a two-year theory sequence. Students take on a historical role and write in the period idiom. The article also describes Eckert's minuet assignment, with minimum lengths for each half — [JMTP, Birson](https://journals.ou.edu/jmtp/ojs/ejournal/article/download/903/1794/1814)
- MIT 21M.303 *Writing in Tonal Forms I* (OCW 2009) assigns a minuet and trio for string quartet in classical style, modelled on Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven. It centres on rounded binary and the key relationship between minuet and trio. Students also write a Lied in Romantic style — [MIT OCW 21M.303](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21m-303-writing-in-tonal-forms-i-spring-2009/pages/syllabus)
- Nicholas Cook, *Analysis Through Composition: Principles of the Classical Style* (1996), uses style composition to teach analysis, with models from J. C. Bach to early Beethoven — [catalogue](https://www.cageweb.be/catalog/orp01:000013912)

### Inferences
- Eckert's six-worksheet ladder (cadences → continuation → melody over bass → fill-in skeleton → complete from 4 bars → whole piece) is close to an app-ready progression. Each rung narrows the student's free choices less than the one before, and the fixed 16-bar 4×4 template can be checked automatically (key area by bar 8, cadence types at bars 8 and 16, Fonte/Monte at bars 9–12).
- "Riepel's error-diagnosis minuet" is a good automated format: show a flawed model and ask the student to find and fix the errors, using multiple choice plus edits.

### Gaps
- Juilliard, Curtis, Eastman and Yale syllabi and textbook adoptions were not found. Kostka & Payne, Clendinning & Marvin, Gauldin's *Counterpoint* and Schubert's *Modal Counterpoint* were not checked against school use in this session.
- Hepokoski & Darcy and Caplin used *for composition* (e.g. "write a Type 3 exposition") was not documented. The only trace is Rabinovitch & Slominski improvising Type 2 and Type 3 sonata second halves (see §3).

## 3. Partimento / schema revival and improvisation-based pedagogy

### Takeaway
Partimento and schema pedagogy now has textbooks (IJzerman 2018, OUP), online corpora (Gjerdingen's Monuments of Partimenti), editions (van Tour and Demeyere's Fenaroli realizations), degree programmes (SCB Basel historical improvisation, under Rudolf Lutz) and research-based classroom trials (Eastman, MTO 2015). The common teaching sequence is: rule of the octave + cadences → schemata as outer-voice skeletons → suspensions/sequences → diminution of a schema skeleton taken from a real piece → partimento realization → short binary piece.

### Cited Findings
- Job IJzerman, *Harmony, Counterpoint, Partimento: A New Method Inspired by Old Masters* (OUP 2018), is a bachelor-level method that combines galant schemata and partimenti and reunites harmony with counterpoint. Its order: the **contrapuntal perspective first** (melody construction, coordination of voices, consonance/dissonance), with chords, inversions, cadences and sequences only later. Its schemata range from parallel thirds/sixths to the omnibus. Exercise modes are playing (realizing schemata at the keyboard, connecting them smoothly), singing, improvising and writing. The online workbook includes partimento exercises and tasks based on existing repertoire. **Each exercise ends with an outline for a composition** — [Conservatorium van Amsterdam](https://www.conservatoriumvanamsterdam.nl/en/about/research/research-projects/curriculum-development-in-music-education/harmony-counterpoint-partimento/)
- IJzerman met Gjerdingen's work in 2007. Gjerdingen is Professor Emeritus at Northwestern — [Nikhil Hogan podcast ep.115](https://sites.libsyn.com/117071/115-job-ijzerman); [AbeBooks listing](https://www.abebooks.com/9780190695002/Harmony-Counterpoint-Partimento-New-Method-0190695005/plp)
- A ZGMTH review notes that IJzerman considers modern partimento and schema didactics "still in their infancy" — [ZGMTH review](https://storage.gmth.de/zgmth/pdf/1049)
- Gjerdingen's "Monuments of Partimenti" website introduces basic improvisation and composition from figured-bass sketches. It has a Beginner's Guide and a Rule of the Octave study, and is based on the 1813 Imbimbo edition of Fenaroli's complete corpus. One source says it gives little verbal instruction, so a teacher is useful — [MusoW entry](https://projects.dharc.unibo.it/musow/view-1635268063-593361); [JMTP article](https://journals.ou.edu/jmtp/article/download/482/1019/1013)
- Fenaroli's corpus: six books of graded partimenti plus a book of rules. Book 4 is described as an advanced course — [Eighteenth-Century Music, "On Fedele Fenaroli's pedagogy"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/eighteenth-century-music/article/on-fedele-fenarolis-pedagogy-an-update/9624A245D7BA5FBC4BD85CE980A6B8B1)
- Schola Cantorum Basiliensis: the historical-improvisation programme combines practical improvisation, history and historical composition, aiming at stylistic range plus thoroughbass and composition knowledge. Its master's programme under Rudolf Lutz is described as pioneering and as having inspired similar programmes in Lyon. Lutz wrote a chapter on using partimenti in teaching in Moelants (ed.), *Partimento and Continuo Playing in Theory and in Practice* — [FHNW](https://fhnw.ch/@@multilingual-selector/317b7b9545a346d3a70a1b10ea3928ce/en); [Ricercare (EAFIT)](https://publicaciones.eafit.edu.co/index.php/ricercare/article/download/4375/3661/16225); [Moelants volume](https://www.sack.de/moelants-partimento-and-continuo-playing-in-theory-and-in-practice/9789461660947)
- Rabinovitch & Slominski, "Towards a Galant Pedagogy" (MTO 21.3, 2015), ran an Eastman experiment in 2013–14 with 18 volunteers, each doing four individual 30–45 minute sessions:
  1. Rule-of-the-octave segments (Durante: 1̂ up to 5̂, 1̂ down to 3̂) with cadences, realized as block chords and then in 2 voices. Then, over a descending bass, a consequent phrase in triple meter from a given motive, then free improvisation.
  2. A schema chart: openings (Meyer), middles (Prinner) and cadences (Cudworth, deceptive, converging, complete). Students choose a path, e.g. Meyer → Prinner → Cudworth, and then build a short binary skeleton.
  3. Suspension patterns, including invertible upper voices, compressed into one polyphonic right-hand line. Then Boccherini's "Ritirata" theme as a diminution of a schema succession, elaborated with given and own motives.
  4. Capstone: a figured-bass adaptation of Durante's first *partimento diminuito*, plus elaborating the skeleton of C. P. E. Bach Wq. 53/1/i.

  Assessment was qualitative only. Observed problems were figured-bass fluency, keyboard skill, dissonance treatment and drifting from the Prinner framework. The authors suggest Roman-numeral or block-chord cues for weaker students. They also improvised Type 2 and Type 3 sonata second halves on C. P. E. Bach Wq. 62/4 — [MTO 21.3](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.15.21.3/mto.15.21.3.rabinovitch.php)
- Peter van Tour and Ewald Demeyere edited Fenaroli realizations (1809 MS) in the series "Monuments of Partimento Realizations" — [Stretta: Fenaroli, Parma manuscript](https://www.stretta-music.pl/fenaroli-the-parma-manuscript-nr-1717204.html)

### Inferences
- The app already has schemata and diminution, so the natural next step is the Rabinovitch–Slominski / IJzerman chain:
  1. A schema-path chooser: pick an opening, a middle and a cadence, and the app assembles the outer voices.
  2. Diminution of a real-piece skeleton, with the original revealed afterwards as a worked example.
  3. Rule of the octave over scale-degree bass segments.
  4. Short Fenaroli/Durante partimenti with automatic checks of upper-voice consonance and figure compliance.
  5. "End each exercise with a composition outline" (IJzerman).
- Because partimento practice is keyboard-oriented, an app could add playback with an "improvise over a loop" mode. Grading improvisation is hard, though, so written realization stays the checkable deliverable.

### Gaps
- Robert Levin and the "Improvisation in the Classical Style" literature, Gjerdingen's Northwestern course content, and Peter van Tour's dissertation *Counterpoint and Partimento* (Uppsala 2015) were not retrieved. All are known but unverified here.

## 4. Form-functional pedagogy, online courses, apps and software (and evidence)

### Takeaway
Free automated species-counterpoint checkers exist and are mature (Artinfuser: species 1–5, 1–9 voices, modes; WesterParse: Westergaard rules on music21; CounterPointer). However, **no controlled study showing they improve learning was found**. The evidence is small, single-institution and descriptive. Form-functional (Caplin) composition assignments appear indirectly: sentence/period and minuet templates in Eckert and MIT.

### Cited Findings
- Artinfuser Exercise (free, since 2017) was reviewed in *College Music Symposium* as "the most robust free species counterpoint website currently available". It supports species 1–5 in 1–9 voices across the diatonic modes, in simple and compound meters — [CMS review](https://symposium.music.org/volume-61-no-2/technology-and-online-resource-reviews-1752877062/a-simple-tool-for-species-counterpoint-drill-and-practice-with-artinfuser-exercise)
- WesterParse (Robert Snarrenberg, WashU) is a Python/music21 parser that tests whether a simple tonal line is valid under Westergaard's species rules. It includes a voice-leading evaluator for simple and combined species and is framed as modelling a competent listener's structural hearing, not as grading — [WashU news](https://music.washu.edu/news/westerparsespecies-counterpoint-online-developed-robert-snarrenberg-associate-prof-theory); [Scitepress 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/104826/)
- The CounterPointer developers note that counterpoint homework has often been rote, requiring "very little skill beyond following a long checklist of rules" — [GitHub counterpointer](https://github.com/counter-pointer/counterpointer)
- FuxCP is a constraint-programming counterpoint generator; a UCLouvain master's thesis works on making it generate "harmonious" counterpoints — [UCLouvain thesis](https://thesis.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/masterthesis/958f395d-c02e-4b4f-a9fa-b195efda1367). CCARH's Stanford Music 254 has a 2024 unit on "Artificial Counterpoint" — [CCARH](https://esf.ccarh.org/Mus254_2024/06B_Artificial-Counterpoint_2024.pdf)
- Evidence on effectiveness, all from ISI Yogyakarta:
  - 2017: Sibelius made counterpoint work more efficient (observation and interviews) — [IAFOR ACTC2017](https://papers.iafor.org/wp-content/uploads/papers/actc2017/ACTC2017_37045.pdf)
  - 2018 action research: "excellent" marks rose from 6/36 to 21/36 across cycles, with no control group.
  - 2020 Promusika study: species-based learning increases effectiveness (qualitative) — [Promusika](https://journal.isi.ac.id/index.php/promusika/article/view/3154)
- A 2024 Nyquist first-species generator paper found the rule set "too strict", and range adjustment complicated — [Scitepress 2024](https://www.scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2024/135158)

### Inferences
- Design lesson: rule checkers alone risk the "checklist" problem that CounterPointer describes. Pair the checker with musical-quality feedback (melodic shape, climax, variety; WesterParse-style structural parsing) and with model comparison.
- Rule strictness should be configurable by tradition (Fux / Jeppesen / Schubert modal; Westergaard tonal; Aldwell–Schachter), as the Nyquist paper's complaint shows.

### Gaps
- Hookpad, Flat, Noteflight, MuseScore-based teaching, Coursera/Berklee/Juilliard Open Studios courses and AI tools were not researched in this session for lack of call budget. No effectiveness studies were found for any of them.
- Hepokoski & Darcy and Caplin as composition curricula: no sources were retrieved.

## 5. Research on composition pedagogy (Webster, Hickey, Kaschub & Smith, deliberate practice, expert process)

### Takeaway
Music-education research (mostly K-12 and teacher education) frames composition as creative thinking. Webster's model (enabling skills + enabling conditions → a Wallas-like preparation, incubation, illumination and verification process → product) is the standard reference. Kaschub & Smith supply a compositional-pedagogy framework. These sources argue for nurturing creative thinking over "training creativity", which supports pairing constraint-based drills with open composition tasks.

### Cited Findings
- *Composing our Future: Preparing Music Educators to Teach Composition*, edited by Kaschub & Smith (OUP 2013), includes Webster's chapter "Music Composition Intelligence and Creative Thinking in Music" (ch. 2, pp. 19–32), which builds on Gardner and Reimer, and Hickey's chapter on what teachers can learn from composition research — [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/36244/chapter/316040867)
- Webster's model of creative thinking in music, traced to Graham Wallas: an intention or idea leads to a product via preparation, incubation, illumination and verification, and requires enabling skills and an enabling environment. It was refined in 2002 — [Nebraska pressbook](https://pressbooks.nebraska.edu/uhon395spr2025/?p=98)
- Hickey & Webster, "Creative Thinking in Music", *Music Educators Journal* (2001), argues that teachers should nurture children's inherent creative musical thinking rather than "train them to be creative" — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2307/3345815)
- Kaschub & Smith, *Minds on Music: Composition for Creative and Critical Thinking* (2009, with NAfME), covers rationale and research, "conceptualizing compositional pedagogy" and composition teaching by grade level — [Presto](https://secure.prestoclassical.co.uk/books/products/8665983--minds-on-music-composition-for-creative-and-critical-thinking)

### Inferences
- For a self-study app, Webster's "verification" stage maps onto automated checks plus playback and self-assessment. "Preparation" maps onto analysis of models, and "incubation and illumination" onto open-ended tasks with fewer constraints. A progression from tight constraints (species) to looser ones (full minuet, Lied) mirrors this.

### Gaps
- No sources were retrieved on deliberate practice in composition or on expert composers' process studies (e.g. Collins's longitudinal studies, Sloboda). Nor were Maud Hickey's *Music Outside the Lines* (2012) or Webster's "Computer-based technology and music teaching and learning" reviews. All are known from background knowledge but unverified here.

## 6. Concrete exercise formats suitable for self-study / automation

### Takeaway
Combining the exam and pedagogy sources gives a ranked list of formats with fixed inputs and checkable outputs. These are the strongest candidates for an app that already does species 1–3, outer-voice harmony, schemata, diminution and sentence/period.

### Cited Findings (formats attested in sources)
- **SATB Bach chorale on a given soprano** (add ATB): Edexcel A-level, Oxford/Manchester, Campobasso, Mainz, MOZ — [Pearson 2019](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A-Level/Music/2016/Exam-materials/59280-gce-a-music-9mu0-02-composition-brief-jun-2019-new.pdf); [MOZ](https://www.moz.ac.at/Downloads-Studierende/Pr%C3%BCfungsbeispiele/musiktheorie_pruefungsbeispiel_ba.pdf)

### Inferences (suggested app additions, ordered by fit with what the app already has)
1. **Rule of the octave + cadence drills**: bass scale-degree segments; the student picks chords and checks the upper voice.
2. **Full SATB chorale on a given soprano** (Bach style). It reuses the existing outer-voice + Roman numeral engine and adds inner voices, spacing, doubling and the Bach cadence formulas. Withheld-answer comparison with the actual Bach setting (Riemenschneider) works as a worked example.
3. **Unfigured bass ("basso dato") with modulation targets** (Italian format), levelled: diatonic → near modulation → suspensions/sequences → distant modulation.
4. **Figured bass realization** with automatic figure-compliance checking, using Fenaroli/Durante partimenti as graded material.
5. **Species 4th/5th + 2-part invention**: the invention opening is given and the student continues with sequence and modulation to V. This also links to the existing diminution module.
6. **Minuet ladder** (Eckert's 6 worksheets) on a fixed 16-bar 4×4 template with schema slots (Fonte/Monte/Ponte, Prinner, cadences). Checkable: bar-8 cadence key, bar-16 PAC, repetition of part-1 material.
7. **Error-diagnosis** (Riepel's Discantista): find and fix the errors in a flawed minuet or chorale. This is cheap to build with existing rule checks.
8. **Schema-path composer** (Meyer → Prinner → cadence) building binary skeletons, then diminution, then comparison with the original (Boccherini, C. P. E. Bach).
9. **Theme and variations / Lied accompaniment / quartet continuation / fugue exposition** as advanced, semi-automated tasks: rule checks on harmony and voice-leading plus self-assessment rubrics and model answers.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found comparing these exercise formats for learning outcomes. The ordering above is inferred from exam progressions and the pedagogical literature, not from experimental evidence.
